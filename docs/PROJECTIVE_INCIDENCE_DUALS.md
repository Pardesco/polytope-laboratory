# Projective and infinite reciprocals

Open **Projective / infinite dual**, choose the reciprocal center and radius, and enter the clipping cube's half-width in source coordinate units. **Save reciprocal view recipe** records the settings in document history and opens the view. **Show saved reciprocal** reconstructs it from the bound finite source. The dialog provides orbit, zoom, canonical directions, a source-linked element selector, clipping-distance changes, an explicit fit of the clipping box, and PNG export. Camera settings are independent of the main viewport and persist with the source view. Undo, history replay and parameter branching retain the original finite model.

The primary [Dual Polyhedra manual](https://software3d.com/Manual/Dual.php?prod=Great) describes ideal vertices for source facial planes through the reciprocal center and a finite user-controlled display distance. The [Morphing manual](https://software3d.com/Manual/Morphing.php?prod=Great) cautions that hemi-polyhedra and infinite duals do not interpolate reliably. This stage provides a projective incidence view, not an infinite-dual morph or printable finite solid.

For a source facial plane `n·(x-c)=d`, its reciprocal is the homogeneous point `[r²n : d]` relative to center `c`. An ideal point has fourth coordinate zero; its direction remains a genuine projective point. It is never replaced by a large finite model coordinate. Plane-fit and center-incidence tolerances are reported; ideal classification is numerical and uncertified. Every literal face has its own reciprocal ID, including distinct coincident plane identities. Every original source edge and cyclic vertex link remains present. The view maps dual vertices to source faces, dual edges to source edges, and dual face cycles to source vertices.

The finite display contract is explicit: finite–finite links use their homogeneous positive-affine segment; finite–ideal links show the **full supporting projective line**, including both affine directions; ideal–ideal links remain at infinity and have no affine occurrence. Lines intersect the clipping cube using their true equations. Cutoff endpoints are display intersections, not new model vertices. Infinite polygon winding and filled regions are unassigned; the view renders wire geometry and retained cyclic incidence. This does not assert a unique Euclidean half-ray interpretation, a closed clipped solid, area, volume or printable net. Self-crossing cycles and coincident reciprocal identities remain an abstract projective incidence complex. Nonordinary repeated/disconnected source links and nonmanifold edges are refused, not repaired.

Actual catalog fixtures include Antiprism U3: 12/24/12 reciprocal vertices/edges/faces, four numerical ideal vertices; and U75: 124/240/60, sixty numerical ideal vertices and 62 coincident plane-identity pairs. Source face cycles, edge IDs, raw RGBA, notes, units and text/PNG annotations remain unchanged in the finite model and recipe snapshots. Reciprocal annotation pixel mapping is unavailable and the dialog states that content stays in the source document.

Saved records bind the complete source attributes and context using a portable numeric digest, so Python and JavaScript JSON number spellings agree without changing raw values. A saved record remains readable after a source edit, but publication recomputes its binding and refuses a stale source, notes, units, metadata or content change. Save a new recipe to bind the edited source. Reconstruction and PNG publication are cancellable; workspace/source changes close and dispose the independent view. Dimensions are not inferred from the clipped drawing.
