# Intrinsic symmetry groups and subgroups

The Evidence inspector computes geometric symmetries from source coordinates and full ordered incidence in intrinsic 2D, 3D and 4D. It does not infer a group from a library name or a shared vertex/edge skeleton. Display rotation, camera projection and surface shrink do not change this computation.

## Search and verification

Every Euclidean symmetry fixes the vertex arithmetic mean. Images of a spanning frame of centered source vertices determine its orthogonal matrix. The search enumerates every Gram-compatible image frame, tests an orthogonal metric action and bijective vertex mapping, then verifies edges, cyclic faces including winding, and cell-face incidence. Exact integer incidence comparisons are batched by face/cell size; they do not use a collision-prone digest.

A complete result requires exhausted frames and verified group closure. Closure uses a generator Cayley traversal: every generator transition remains in the verified action set, and words in these generators reach every listed action. Verified orthogonal actions agreeing on the spanning frame agree on all vertices. This avoids an order-squared multiplication table. The identity, generator indices and transition count are recorded.

The domain is a full-dimensional source realization with at most 1,000 vertices. Candidate frames are limited to 1-1,000,000, with a default of 100,000; search nodes have a separate bound of ten times the frame limit plus dimension. A resource limit returns verified actions and an explicit incomplete status, without group/orbit/stabilizer claims. Degenerate ambient realizations and ambiguous repeated face cycles/cell boundaries are diagnosed. This is a geometric group, not a general combinatorial automorphism computation.

| 600-cell | 14,400 | 7,200 | 120 / 720 / 1,200 / 600 |
| 120-cell | 14,400 | 7,200 | 600 / 1,200 / 720 / 120 |

Expected orders come from the simplex/hypercube formulas and exceptional regular-polytope constructions in [Gert Heckman's Coxeter Groups, section 1.3](https://www.math.ru.nl/~heckman/CoxeterGroups.pdf#page=14), including the 24-cell construction and duality of the 600-/120-cell. Tests verify source coordinates and complete incidence independently of these expected order values. All six references have one orbit in each entity dimension. Rotated, translated and scaled 24-cells, unequal-axis 4D blocks, asymmetric 4D simplices and same-skeleton cell-incidence changes provide non-catalog fixtures.

## Restrictions and actions

Proper rotations restrict to determinant +1, including general double rotations in 4D. A setwise entity stabilizer fixes the selected vertex, edge, face or cell as an entity; its vertices need not be fixed individually. These restrictions can be combined.

User-supplied source generator IDs define the subgroup generated by those verified full-source actions. Blank UI input requests the full chosen restriction, `identity` requests the trivial group, and comma-separated IDs request up to 64 distinct source generators. Generator IDs refer to the deterministic unrestricted result for this source and algorithm version; they are not portable names for transformations across different geometry or algorithms. A generator violating the requested orientation/stabilizer is refused. Generator-defined groups require an exhausted complete source search. Geometry operations clear manual generator-ID preferences.

Each action reports an intrinsic matrix, determinant, vertex permutation and source action ID. Its affine action is x' = center + matrix (x - center). The action inspector shows these values; orbit inspection highlights the source vertices belonging to the selected entity orbit. Complete results include vertex/edge/face/cell orbit IDs, sizes and setwise stabilizer orders relative to the returned group. Restricted results distinguish their own order from the unrestricted verified source order. Inspection preferences persist in the document snapshot; action caches remain transient and stale asynchronous responses are discarded.

Signed coordinate permutations remain an explicit comparison method. Their verified subgroup can be smaller: a standard 24-cell has 384 signed-coordinate actions versus 1,152 full geometric actions. Their full-group claim still requires a verified cube/cross metric pattern and known order. Geometric restriction options are not silently accepted by the signed-axis method.

## Stellation and numeric limits

Finite enclosed-region searches accept proper source rotations and user-generated subgroups. Actions must also preserve the actual observed region geometry/incidence, and their region permutations must close. Output distinguishes the full source group, selected source subgroup and observation-domain restriction. An octahedron's single coordinate reflection yields four paired outer-region orbits and the seed orbit, so 32 subsets produce 16 seed-containing connected candidates; this differs from full-group enumeration. These remain explicit finite-region criteria, not the classic stellation classification or full benchmark reflexibility semantics.

All metric/orthogonal comparisons use normalized float64 predicates at declared tolerance 8e-8. Group completeness is numerical for the supplied coordinates, including rational-certified models. No exact algebraic/interval certificate, larger-domain completeness or hands-on benchmark qualification is implied. Headless symmetry defaults to geometric; options include method, max_frames, orientation, stabilize and generator_ids. Stellation accepts these geometric restrictions through symmetry_options. Tests live in tests/test_symmetry.py and tests/test_stellation.py; desktop/portable smoke checks exercise the actual bundled engine and inspector.
