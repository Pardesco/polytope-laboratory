# Source content during dual morphs

Text labels now follow declared native source ownership during supported morphs.
Repeated output instances each receive their source label. Reciprocal endpoints
can change its rank: a source face can own a dual vertex, for example. Generic
incident-face lists are insufficient to establish ownership. Missing ownership
prevents the proposed frame from being displayed.

Text on a face without an image becomes a moving billboard. A face PNG requires
an actual output face with all of its original ordered source corners and a
verified affine tangent sheet. Clipped, subdivided, nonaffine, collapsed and
rank-changed image sheets are diagnosed. Some expansion and sizing poses retain
these sheets; the reciprocal endpoint of a source face generally does not.

Mapping, native descriptors and image decoding are prepared on detached geometry
before the live frame changes. Its camera objects are independently owned.
Unsupported mappings, cancellation or changed source/content leave the prior
frame and content intact. Billboard canvases retain readable vertical orientation;
face textures keep their source-frame UV orientation.

Native projects retain the source annotations, enabled morph, notes and units.
PNG capture waits for content readiness. Existing unsupported tour/keyframe,
mask, shrink and explosion combinations retain their own diagnostics.

Work limits are 1,024 entries, 4,096 mapped occurrences, 32,000 owner records per
rank and one million traversed incidences, alongside existing canvas/asset limits.
Five focused production groups cover all eight 3D methods, reciprocal endpoints,
4D expansion labels, affine PNG sheets and stale/unsupported ownership. An actual
app check covers moving text at two poses, PNG capture, enabled-morph Save/Open
and refusal of an unsupported PNG endpoint while retaining the previous pose.
