# Stereographic surface repair in 0.18

Independent adaptive refinement in 0.17 could give neighbouring surface triangles different subdivisions of the same curved edge. A coarse chord then met two finer chords, leaving a visible crease or gap despite compatible analytic normals. The defect occurred across source-face fan diagonals and inside a parent triangle; matching endpoint sets alone was insufficient to verify the mesh.

The production worker now closes refinement over shared presentation edges before replacing the mesh. Red/green subdivisions retain matching boundary segments, concave face fills, source face/cell owners and analytic spherical normals. Depth, mesh, visit and sample-cache budgets are checked before each replacement. When refinement cannot finish, the last conforming mesh retains explicit clipped or unresolved holes rather than claiming the whole requested surface is present. The validated synchronous fallback uses the same prepared typed geometry and computation as the worker.

Indexed corners preserve presentation-instance identity. Virtual star-fill corners have explicit IDs within their source face and cell domain; they are approximate paint-mesh identities, **not native incidence vertices**. Their existing normalized planar coincidence tolerance is `1e-9`. Triangles whose corner IDs collapse during this paint canonicalization are excluded as degenerate. Shrink and translated entity instances retain separate domains. No global source welding, hull substitution, changes to native coordinates/cycles, or changes to stored RGBA, units and notes are involved. Point and edge projection buffers remain unchanged.

The independent headless suites [surface conformity](../tests/stereographic-surface-conformity.test.mjs) and [virtual conformity](../tests/stereographic-virtual-conformity.test.mjs) pass **12 tests**. They compare actual leaf-edge intervals, pair interior edges, verify normals and ownership, exercise signed `5/2`, `5/3`, `5/-2` and both winding fills, check shrink and explicit generalized translated entities, and verify source RGBA, source immutability, unchanged point/edge buffers and incomplete resource-limit results. Read-only raw arrangement probes for `5/2`, `5/3`, `7/2`, `7/3` and `8/3` at three angular phases found no remaining unpaired interior edges after the degenerate filter.

The actual development Electron/GPU run [ikuH4k](../artifacts/stereographic-surface-seams-ikuH4k/result.json) passed with zero page errors. The opaque and translucent stereographic quad each had 30 matching shared-diagonal samples on both sides and maximum normal difference zero. The sampled interior GPU bands recorded:

| Presentation | Minimum luminance | Maximum adjacent jump |
|---|---:|---:|
| Opaque | 66 | 1 |
| Translucent, opacity 0.45 | 44 | 0.667 |

Bands cover diagonal parameters `0.15..0.65`, including the old mismatch at `0.21875`; the independent mesh check covers the entire diagonal. The earlier [UqGSRM run](../artifacts/stereographic-surface-seams-UqGSRM/result.json) is preserved: its endpoint bands crossed the sphere silhouette and produced a false seam failure. The successful run also preserves native source attributes and observer camera across perspective/stereographic captures and tests opaque/translucent tesseract rotation. This is observed development-runtime evidence, not packaged-release qualification or a universal GPU result.

The actual packaged 0.18 candidate also passed [the surface GPU regression](../artifacts/desktop-qualification-Y68eUf/surfaceSeams/stereographic-surface-seams-4CnVZf/result.json), using its bundled renderer and native engine. All 35 desktop scenarios and 863 registered frontend tests pass. The exact candidate was promoted as [the 0.18 portable](../release/Polytope%20Laboratory%200.18.0.exe), with development Python unavailable and all 3,064 linked-source hashes verified. [Release evidence](../artifacts/release-0.18.0.json) explicitly records reuse of the unchanged 0.17 native qualification.

Capture requires the current source/pose and a complete requested conforming surface. Worker output and transport both reject a complete-surface claim containing pole-excluded leaves; unresolved or omitted primitives also refuse capture. The synchronous fallback enforces the same surface-completeness gate. Existing point/edge projection-domain clipping remains a separate diagnosed policy.

Limits remain explicit: at most 8,192 supported input surface triangles, 100,000 mesh leaves, subdivision depth eight, 1,000,000 classification visits, 500,000 cached samples and 64 closure rounds; typed transport buffers are bounded to 32 MiB per snapshot. These limits can leave partial previews or refuse capture. Float64 pole/quality predicates and Float32 display buffers are approximate. The error criterion samples edge midpoints and triangle interiors; it does not bound the maximum error over every curved patch. Orthographic pixel conversion qualifies that sampled criterion; perspective has no depth-independent pixel guarantee. Dense motion can remain slow, and overlapping transparent surfaces retain ordinary GPU ordering limitations. The finite mesh and pixel observations above do not establish whole-surface, transparency-ordering or universal hardware conformance.

The final qualification combines 24 and three actually executed passing prefixes with seven and one passing rerun scenarios on identical executable/archive bytes. Original failed reports remain preserved. The first worker fixture used the older 2,000-input cutoff; its replacement stays within the 20,000 native vertex limit and exceeds the current 8,192 surface-input limit. The layer harness settles pending controls before Open; the production workspace-change guard remains unchanged.
